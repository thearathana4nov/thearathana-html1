    Pchum Ben is one of the most important traditional festivals in Cambodia. It is also known as Ancestors' Day. This festival lasts for 15 days and usually takes place in September or October. During Pchum Ben, Cambodian people visit pagodas to pray, offer food to Buddhist monks, and remember their deceased parents, grandparents, and ancestors. Some people prepare traditional food, such as Bay Ben, which is made from rice. They believe that doing good deeds and making religious offerings can bring merit to their deceased relatives. Pchum Ben is also a special time for families to reunite, share food, and spend time together. This festival teaches us to respect our ancestors, love our families, help other people, and preserve our Cambodian traditions. In conclusion, Pchum Ben is not only an important religious festival but also a meaningful part of Cambodian culture.
